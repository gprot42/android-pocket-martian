Pocket Martian privacy policy

Last updated 5 October 2026

Pocket Martian is an Android app for chatting with xAI's Grok models using your own xAI API key. It is an independent app, not made by or affiliated with xAI. This page explains what the app does with your information.

The short version

  • The developer of Pocket Martian receives none of your data. The app has no accounts, analytics, advertising or crash reporting.
  • What you ask goes to xAI, the company behind Grok, because answering it is the point of the app. You pay for that with your own API key, under xAI's privacy policy.
  • Everything else stays on your phone.

What goes to xAI

When you use these features, the app sends the following to xAI's API (api.x.ai) over an encrypted connection:

  • Your prompts and the conversation so far, so Grok can answer in context.
  • Photos and scanned pages you attach. Scans are processed on your phone; only if the phone can't find the page's edges is the photo sent to Grok to find them.
  • Your voice, when you dictate a prompt or use the voice translator, to be turned into text or translated speech.
  • Your approximate location and nearby place name, only when location is switched on in the app's settings and you have given Android permission, so Grok can answer questions like "what's the weather here". You can turn this off at any time.

The app asks xAI not to store its responses (store=false). xAI's own handling of what it receives is set out in its privacy policy and terms.

If you use the optional credits and usage screen, a management key you provide is sent to xAI's management API (management-api.x.ai). If you use the experimental SuperGrok sign-in, you sign in on xAI's own pages (auth.x.ai).

Other services the app talks to

  • Realtime Trains (data.rtt.io): only if you add your own Realtime Trains token. The station and train being asked about are sent with your token to fetch live UK train times.
  • Your phone's geocoding service: with location on, Android's built-in geocoder turns coordinates into a place name. Which company provides it depends on your phone; on many phones it is Google.
  • Public DNS resolvers (Google, dns.google, and Cloudflare, cloudflare-dns.com): only if your network can't look up api.x.ai, the app asks them for that address instead. Only the name api.x.ai is sent.
  • GitHub (api.github.com): the version installed from GitHub, not the one from Google Play, checks once a day for a newer release. GitHub sees the app's version number and your IP address, as with any web request.
  • Links you open, such as sources under an answer, open in your browser and are covered by those websites' own policies.

What stays on your phone

Your xAI API key, any Realtime Trains token or management key, your settings, saved conversations, the response history and the app's diagnostic logs are kept in the app's private storage, which other apps can't read. They are left out of Android's cloud backups. Clear in the app deletes the saved conversation, and uninstalling the app deletes everything it stored.

Permissions

  • Microphone: for dictation and the voice translator, only while you are using them.
  • Location: optional, as described above, and only while the app is open.
  • Internet: to reach the services above.

Photos reach the app only when you pick or share them, or take one with your camera app; it has no permission to read your photo library or use the camera itself.

Children

Pocket Martian is meant for adults and is not directed at children.

Changes

If the app's handling of data changes, this page will be updated and the date at the top changed. The page's history is public in the app's GitHub repository.

Contact

Questions about privacy: open an issue at github.com/gprot42/android-pocket-martian/issues.
